# Café Rush — Brand Strategy

**Version** 1.0
**Date** 2026-09-16
**Status** Approved as the source of truth for all brand, marketing and store output

This document governs every word and every colour that leaves the project with the name
Café Rush on it. Where it conflicts with a marketing instinct, this document wins. Where it
conflicts with `docs/requirements.md`, `docs/target-player.md`, `docs/art-plan.md`,
`docs/design-spec.md` or `docs/rush-pivot.md`, those documents win and this one is wrong and
must be amended.

---

## 1. The one-line promise

> **A ninety-second cooking rush you buy once and own forever.**

That sentence has three jobs. **Ninety-second cooking rush** tells a genre-literate player
exactly what the game is — seats filling, patience draining, machines running on their own
timers behind you — so nobody arrives expecting a calm farming sim and leaves a one-star
review saying so. **Buy once** names the whole commercial model in two words: a free first
chapter, then one payment of AED 24.99, and nothing else is ever for sale. **Own forever**
is the longevity claim and the anti-FOMO claim at the same time: thirty days, ninety stars
and an Endless Rush that does not end, none of it expiring, none of it requiring you to show
up on a Tuesday.

It rules out three things we will be tempted by. It rules out calling this a cozy game — the
player in `docs/target-player.md` liked the fast one, and mis-selling the pace would put the
game in front of exactly the wrong audience. It rules out a second purchase, a currency, a
season or a discount countdown, because "once" and "forever" are the promise and the first
expiring offer makes us liars. And it rules out selling on production value: the art was
generated rather than commissioned and the promise is deliberately about the deal and the
loop, not about how the croissant is painted.

## 2. Positioning statement

> **For the lapsed Dash player who finished the cooking games they loved and was
> disappointed that they ended, Café Rush is the premium time-management game that gives you
> the whole rush — thirty days, twelve recipes and an endless shift — for one payment,
> unlike Cooking Fever, Cooking Madness and every frenzy clone whose pacing exists to sell
> gems, because a shift tuned to be fair pays for itself at AED 24.99 and a shift tuned to
> be frustrating pays for itself in gems.**

**For the lapsed Dash player.** Not "casual mobile gamers". `docs/target-player.md` names a
specific person: someone who plays story, cooking, farming, building and tycoon games, who
played the Dash series, liked it, and whose single loudest complaint was that it was
"finished pretty fast". Longevity is the headline want, and it is named as the failure of
the closest existing product. We position against that disappointment, not against a
demographic.

**Is the premium time-management game.** Not "cooking game", which is a shelf so crowded the
word carries no meaning, and not "cozy game", which is a genre this product is not in.
Time-management is the accurate frame — it is what Diner Dash invented and what
*Cook, Serve, Delicious!* proved can be sold premium. "Premium" is doing load-bearing work
in the category name because the price model is the differentiator.

**That gives you the whole rush for one payment.** The benefit is completeness. Thirty days
in five chapters, twelve recipes from espresso to saffron karak, eight upgrade lines and
Endless Rush — all of it behind one door, opened once. The player is not buying content;
they are buying the absence of further transactions.

**Unlike Cooking Fever, Cooking Madness and every frenzy clone.** We name the real
competitors rather than a polite abstraction. Their download numbers are free-install
numbers and do not transfer to willingness to pay (`docs/rush-pivot.md`), so we are not
competing with them for volume. We are competing for the specific person who has already
uninstalled one of them in irritation.

**Because a shift tuned to be fair pays for itself at AED 24.99.** This is the reason to
believe, and it is a business fact rather than a claim about our character. Free-to-play
frenzy pacing is frustrating by design because frustration is the product being sold. We
took the money up front, so we have no reason to make day nineteen unfair — and we held the
art cost to nearly nothing (`docs/art-plan.md` route 1, generated rather than commissioned)
specifically so that a premium price could carry the game. The absence of gems is not restraint. It is arithmetic.

## 3. Who this is for

She has a phone full of games she has stopped opening. She liked Diner Dash — genuinely
liked it, played it properly, got fast at it — and when it ended she went looking for the
next one and found a wall of games that looked the same and behaved completely differently.
One of them made her wait four hours for a kitchen to be built. One of them sold a
gem-purchased speed boost that was, in practice, the only way to clear level thirty-one. One
of them wanted her to ask three friends for a key. Two of them played a thirty-second
unskippable video between shifts. She uninstalled all of them, and what she wrote afterwards
was not a rant about capitalism. It was a request: something in this genre, that lasts a
while, that does not require her to show up.

She plays a few hours a week. Sometimes a fortnight passes and she does not open a game at
all, and she needs that to cost her nothing — no broken streak, no expired event, no
withered crops. She will pay. She said so plainly, and she framed it the way most people do:
a few dollars, to make the ads go away. She is not a whale and will never be one. She is
worth exactly one purchase, and the entire business depends on that purchase being a good
enough experience that she buys the next game too — which is what the Kairosoft owner in the
same thread, with five of their titles on his phone, tells us about this audience.

She is fast. This matters more than anything else on this page and it is the thing we will
most often get wrong. The genre she is asking for is the frenzy genre. She wants overlapping
timers, a full counter, a customer going red at seat three while the dough is still four
seconds out. She did not reject the pace. She rejected the scaffolding bolted to it.

**She is not** a cozy-game player who wants a gentle evening — she asked for the Dash games
by name. She is not a completionist who will grind ninety stars out of obligation; she will
grind them because the run is good. She is not a whale, a subscriber, a daily-active-user, a
retention cohort or a funnel. She is not a child, and she is not a lapsed player we get to
win back with a notification. And she is not waiting for us. If day nineteen is unfair or
day six ends in a pop-up, she will close the app and never think about it again.

## 4. What we stand against

Café Rush refuses, permanently and in the shipped binary:

**Energy, stamina and lives that refill in real time.** A ninety-second shift behind a
four-hour meter is the specific insult that started this project. Any shift can be replayed
immediately, any number of times, forever.

**Build queues and real-time waits.** Every upgrade applies the instant it is bought.
Nothing completes in 2h.

**Premium currency.** One currency, AED, earned by playing. It cannot be bought, gifted,
topped up or traded.

**Paywalled difficulty.** No purchase, no ad, no consumable and no boost may make a shift
easier, extend a timer, skip a day or raise earnings. This one constraint deletes the entire
frenzy-genre monetisation playbook, which is the point of it.

**Paywalled accessibility.** Relaxed Timing is free, always available, and earns full stars
and full progression. A game that charges for the easing of difficulty has simply moved the
same offence one room over.

**Social gating.** No invites, no keys from friends, no guild requirement, no
share-to-continue.

**Ads.** Not interstitial, not banner, not rewarded, not "optional" rewarded. The binary
contains no ad SDK. Her stated willingness to pay was payment *in exchange for* their
absence; a rewarded-ad button breaks the deal we took her money on.

**Daily rewards, streaks, events and expiring content.** Anything earnable today is earnable
in six months, identically.

**Notifications that ask her to come back.** Off by default. The only permitted notification
is a local reminder she set herself, in words she chose.

**Discount countdowns and launch-week urgency.** The price is the price.

The refusal is the brand rather than a feature list because every one of those patterns is
individually profitable, and a brand is only what you turn down when it costs you something.
A feature list is a set of things we built. This is a set of things we were offered and
declined — a rewarded-ad button is perhaps three days of work and a measurable revenue line,
and we will not ship it in year four either. The player cannot verify our intentions, but she
can verify a binary with no ad SDK in it, and she can verify that the store page still says
one purchase eighteen months after launch. That verifiability is the asset. It is also
fragile in exactly one direction: it survives a bad review, and it does not survive us doing
it once.

There is no sentiment in this. We are not protecting her from the industry. We picked a
business model where our incentives and hers point the same way, and then we removed our own
ability to change our minds.

## 5. Brand personality

**Fast.** The product's first quality: overlapping timers, a counter that fills, ninety
seconds that feel shorter than ninety seconds. Everything we make should move at that pace,
including the sentences. *Often confused with:* frantic. Frantic is chaos the player cannot
read. Fast is chaos the player can read and is only just keeping up with.

**Plain.** We say the number, the price, the recipe and the reason. A hint tells you what to
do; a failure card tells you what happened. *Often confused with:* blunt. Blunt is telling a
player they were too slow. Plain is telling them the dough takes five seconds.

**Warm.** The café is lamp-lit at dusk, the palette is amber on ink, and the street outside
is a real place with karak and luqaimat on it. Warmth is in the light and the food, never in
the tone of voice. *Often confused with:* cozy. Cozy is a promise of no pressure, and this
game is made of pressure. We are warm and difficult at once, which is what a good kitchen is.

**Specific.** Regag rolls, not "flatbread snack". Saffron karak, not "premium tea". Ninety
seconds, not "fast-paced action". AED 24.99, not "affordable". Specificity is how a small
game sounds confident next to a big one. *Often confused with:* exotic. We are not
presenting the UAE as a setting to be marvelled at; it is simply where the shop is, and a
regag roll needs no more explanation than a croissant.

**Honest.** We state what the game does not do — no in-shift screen-reader support in v1.0,
thirty days and then Endless, thirty minutes free. Naming our limits is the same instinct
that made us name our refusals. *Often confused with:* humble. We are not apologising for
the scope. We chose it, and we will defend it.

## 6. Voice and tone

### The rules

1. **British English**, sentence case, Oxford comma off. Serial commas only where meaning
   requires them.
2. **Second person, present tense.** "You hold the milk", not "players can hold milk".
3. **No exclamation marks in any player-facing string**, including result cards and purchase
   confirmations. The game's excitement is in the clock, not the punctuation.
4. **No hype adjectives.** Amazing, epic, ultimate, addictive, insane, crazy, satisfying,
   relaxing and stunning are banned words. If a line survives their deletion, it did not
   need them.
5. **Numbers are real and specific.** 90 seconds, 30 days, 12 recipes, 8 machines, AED 24.99.
   Never "hundreds of levels", never a number we have not counted.
6. **Never imply urgency the game does not have.** No "now", "today", "don't miss", "limited",
   "hurry". The only clock in this product runs inside a shift.
7. **Failure copy is diagnostic.** Name what happened, in the player's own run, in one line.
   Never scold, never console, never say "oops".
8. **Never ask the player to come back**, in any channel, ever.
9. **Local words stand on their own.** Karak, luqaimat, regag, istikana. Glossed exactly once
   in the recipe list; never glossed again, never italicised, never in quotes.
10. **Length.** UI lines are twelve words or fewer. Store paragraphs are four sentences or
    fewer. Hints are one sentence.
11. **ALL CAPS is a typographic style, not a voice.** Only the 9 pt `label` style is
    uppercase — DAY, EARNED, TARGET, STREAK. Nothing else shouts.
12. **Arabic is not a translation of the voice, it is the voice in Arabic.** Dish names use
    the local term — كرك, لقيمات, رقاق — reviewed by a Gulf-Arabic speaker. The Arabic build
    is never the afterthought build.

### Before and after

| Context | Wrong | Right |

## 7. Naming

**Verdict: keep Café Rush. The subtitle carries the load.**

**What it gets right.** Two words, five syllables, and both halves do work. *Café* places the
setting and signals food without claiming a cuisine, which matters when the menu is karak and
luqaimat rather than burgers. *Rush* is the honest word for the loop and the correction the
project needed — `docs/rush-pivot.md` is the document where this game stopped being calm, and
the name should not quietly re-open that argument. Together they read as a place and a state,
which is exactly the product: a small café, ninety seconds at a time. It is pronounceable in
Arabic and English, it is short enough for a 48 dp app icon at two lines, and it sits
comfortably in the display face.

**What it risks.** Genre crowding is real and we should not pretend otherwise: *rush*, *dash*,
*fever*, *frenzy* and *madness* are the standard vocabulary of this shelf, and a search for
"café rush" will surface neighbours. Searchability is the sharper problem — nobody searches
for a name they have never heard, so the name contributes almost nothing to discovery on its
own. The accented *é* is a minor typing friction; store search normalises diacritics, so we
keep it, because "Cafe Rush" looks like a typo and the é is a quiet promise that the details
were attended to.

**Why we keep it anyway.** A distinctive name that nobody searches is a worse problem than a
familiar name in a crowded field, because the name is not the discovery mechanism — the
subtitle, the keywords and the screenshots are. Renaming buys differentiation we cannot spend
and costs us every asset, file path and prototype reference already carrying the name. The
crowding is survivable precisely because our differentiator is not the name: it is the price
model, and that goes in the subtitle where it is indexed and read.

**What the subtitle must do.** Three jobs, in this order: state the commercial model, because
that is the only thing about this product that a competitor's listing cannot also claim;
carry the anti-brand in words a scanning player recognises from their own frustration; and
say nothing about quality, because every listing on the shelf says that and none of it is
read. It must not describe the gameplay — the title and the first screenshot already do —
and it must not contain an adjective.

**Exact strings:**

| Field | String | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Apple App Store title (≤ 30) | `Café Rush: Cooking Game` | 23 |
| Apple App Store subtitle (≤ 30) | `No ads. No timers. One price.` | 29 |

The word "Cooking" is in the title for one reason: the title field is a search index, not a
headline. The subtitle is the headline, and it is the only line in this document permitted to
sell by negation.

## 9. Typography

Three Latin faces, two Arabic faces, all SIL Open Font License. The brand type system is the
game's type system extended upward, so a store screenshot and a headline never look like two
companies.

| Role | Face | Weights | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark and display | Bricolage Grotesque | 800 | The wordmark, headlines, screen titles, money totals, button labels, floating payments |
| Display secondary | Bricolage Grotesque | 600 | Subheads and standfirsts only, where 800 at that size would dominate the headline above it |
| Body and UI | DM Sans | 400 / 500 / 700 | Running copy, hints, dish names, shop descriptions, settings, store descriptions, documents |
| Numerals and micro-labels | DM Mono | 400 / 500 | Clocks, timers, stock counts, prices, all `label`-style uppercase |
| Arabic display | Cairo | 700 / 900 | The Arabic wordmark and all Arabic headlines |
| Arabic body and numerals | IBM Plex Sans Arabic | 400 / 500 / 600 | All Arabic running copy and numerals, with `font-feature-settings: "tnum"` |

**The size relationship.** The in-game scale is authoritative and brand material extends it
at the same ratios: `display-xl` 34/36, `display-l` 29/31, `display-m` 22/24, `display-s`
17/20 in Bricolage 800; `body-l` 15/22, `body-m` 13/19, `body-s` 11.5/17 in DM Sans 400;
`num-l` 20/22, `num-m` 14/18, `num-s` 11/14 in DM Mono; `label` 9/12 at +0.14em uppercase.
Above `display-xl`, brand headlines continue the ratio by multiplying: 34 → 48 → 68 → 96,
line-height at 1.05 throughout, and nothing between those steps. Below `label`, nothing.

**Rules for headline setting.**

1. Bricolage 800, tracking −0.02em at 22 pt and above, −0.01em from 17 to 21 pt, 0 below.
   Never positive tracking in Bricolage.
2. Maximum two lines. A headline that needs three is a headline that needs cutting.
3. Ragged right, never justified, never centred over more than one line. Break lines on
   meaning, not on width — "A ninety-second cooking rush / you buy once and own forever."
4. Sentence case always. The wordmark is "Café Rush" with the é, in both cases and in every
   size.
5. Never all-caps, never letter-spaced, never outlined, never stretched, never in a gradient,
   never with a drop shadow. The one permitted effect is a `sun-300` glow behind the wordmark
   on key art, at 12% or less.
6. `cream-50` on `ink-900`, or `ink-900` on `paper-100`. Amber headlines only when the
   headline is one word.
7. Numerals inside a headline stay in Bricolage. Numerals that are data — a price, a clock, a
   count — switch to DM Mono, always. A price in a headline is data.
8. Arabic headlines set in Cairo 900 at the same optical weight as Bricolage 800, never
   lighter. Latin and Arabic lockups are built as two designs, not one flipped.
9. Minimum rendered size anywhere, in any medium: 9 pt, and only for `label`. Nothing a
   player must read while the clock runs is below 11 pt.

**Subtitle (29/30)**


**Promotional text (165/170)**

```
The first chapter is free. If the ninety seconds grab you, one payment of AED 24.99 opens the other twenty-four days. No ads, no gems, no energy, no second purchase.
```

**Description — opening paragraph**

```
Ninety seconds. Five seats filling at once. The dough takes five of those seconds, which
means you start it before anyone has ordered it — and that is the whole game. Machines cook
ingredients on their own timers into bins behind you; dishes are assembled from that stock
in an instant. Being fast is not the skill. Holding the right thing before it is asked for

```

**Description — body**

```
You run a small café on a shopping street in the UAE. Karak chai, luqaimat, regag rolls,
saffron karak, and nine more recipes across thirty days. Customers sit down, order up to
three things, and lose patience at their own pace. The counter holds only a few plates and a full
counter blocks you, so starting the wrong machine is a mistake, not a delay.

Thirty days in five chapters. Each chapter adds something the last one could not do:
regulars with a usual order, food that goes cold, customers who change their mind, a machine
that breaks mid-shift. Day nineteen is not day four with a faster clock.
```

## 11. Brand rules

The list that holds when someone is in a hurry. Every line is a veto, not a preference.

**Commercial — these are contractual, not stylistic**

1. There is never a second thing for sale. One non-consumable at AED 24.99, forever. Paid
   expansions, if they ever ship, are self-contained, separately titled, and never required
   to finish what a player already owns.
2. No ad SDK is ever linked into the binary, including for attribution or analytics.
3. No premium currency, no consumable, no boost, no subscription, no season, no battle pass.
4. Nothing purchasable ever makes a shift easier, and nothing that makes a shift easier is
   ever purchasable.
5. No discount countdown, no launch-week urgency, no "price rises soon". A price change is
   announced plainly and without a deadline.
6. No daily reward, streak, event, or expiring content of any kind. If it can be earned
   today, it can be earned in six months, identically.
7. No notification that asks a player to come back. Notifications are off by default and only
   ever a reminder the player set themselves.
8. No leaderboard, invite, share-to-continue, account requirement or online dependency.
9. The store listing never states a claim the binary does not honour, and never omits a
   limitation to make the listing cleaner.

**Verbal**

10. No exclamation marks in player-facing strings. None.
11. The banned words are banned in every channel: amazing, epic, ultimate, addictive, insane,
    crazy, satisfying, relaxing, stunning, unlock the fun, get ready.
12. The game is never described as cozy, casual, relaxing or calm. It is a rush.
13. No invented number ever appears — no download counts, no ratings we have not received, no
    market statistics, no "loved by thousands".
14. Failure copy never scolds, consoles or apologises. It says what happened.
15. Local dish names are never glossed twice, never italicised, never framed as exotic.
16. The Arabic build is never shipped late, never machine-translated, and never shown with
    English screenshots.

**Visual**

17. A hex value that is not a `docs/design-spec.md` §2.3 token — or `terracotta-400` /
    `paper-100` from §8 above — never appears in any asset, in the game or out of it.
18. `brass-400` is money and nothing else. `ok-500` is money earned and target met and nothing
    else. `state-urgent` never touches food, a resting button or a marketing field.
19. `ink-outline` `#2A1D16` is the only stroke colour on any artwork anywhere.
20. The wordmark is Bricolage Grotesque 800, sentence case, with the é. It is never all-caps,
    never letter-spaced, never outlined, never stretched, never gradiented, never shadowed,
    and never placed on a ground other than `ink-900`, `ink-950` or `paper-100`.
21. Headlines are never longer than two lines and never centred over more than one.
22. No screenshot is ever mocked up, upscaled, composited from art we do not ship, or taken
    from a build with features the store version does not have. What is on the store page is
    what installs.
23. Marketing art is the shipped art. Every dish, ingredient, machine, portrait and room in
    a banner, screenshot or press image is a file from `art/sprites/`, at or below its
    rendered size in the build, composited on a `docs/design-spec.md` ground. No stock
    photography, no photographic food, no separately commissioned key art, and nothing drawn
    at a fidelity the game does not contain. *(Amended 2026-09-16: the shipped set of 41
    assets is image-generated painted illustration in the Cooking-Fever register, including
    twelve painted customer portraits — see `art/art-brief.md`. The original rule banned
    that outright and was wrong about the product. What survives, and is the point, is that
    the listing may never show art the build does not have.)*
24. Nothing flashes more than three times a second, in the game or in a trailer.
25. Nothing ever renders below 9 pt, and nothing below 11 pt that a player must read while the
    clock runs.

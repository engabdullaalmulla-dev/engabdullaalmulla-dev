Café Rush · art generation

Prompts for the whole set

Forty-one assets, each written so the set holds together rather than looking like forty-one separate pictures. The prompts matter less than the method — read the six steps first, because getting consistency is the entire difficulty here, and no prompt alone solves it.

The method

Follow this order. Generating assets one by one in any order is exactly how you end up with twelve drinks that do not belong on the same counter.
01
Make the anchor first, and only the anchor Generate the Latte and nothing else. Iterate on it until you would ship it. Every other asset in the game is going to be matched to this one image, so a mediocre anchor contaminates all forty-one. Budget most of your time here.
02
Lock the style stem, never edit it The long block below is invariant. Copy it once. From then on the only thing that changes between prompts is the short subject clause. If you find yourself improving the stem halfway through the set, you have to regenerate everything before it.
03
Feed the anchor back as a style reference This is the step that actually produces consistency. Midjourney: upload the anchor and pass --sref <url> --sw 100 on every later prompt. Stable Diffusion: IP-Adapter with the anchor as the reference image, weight ~0.6. Without this the stem alone will drift.
04
Generate the whole set in one sitting Model versions change under you. A set produced across three weeks will not match a set produced in one afternoon, even with identical prompts.
05
Judge them as a row, never one at a time Put all twelve dishes side by side at 64px before you accept any of them. An asset that looks good alone but sits at a different scale or light angle is the failure this whole procedure exists to prevent.
06
Characters last, and expect them to hurt Faces are where generators are least consistent and most obviously wrong. Do the food first so you have a working set before you spend patience here.

The style stem

Every food and object prompt is this block followed by one subject clause. It fixes the rendering style, the camera, the light and the background — the four things that must never vary.
Why a grey background rather than transparent: image generators are unreliable at true alpha. A flat mid-grey with no gradient keys out cleanly in any background remover, and it keeps the generator from inventing a scene behind the object.

Ingredients

Eight. These appear in the machine bins at 34px and inside order chips at 20px, so the silhouette has to survive being small. Tap any row for the full prompt.

Dishes

Twelve — the six built plus the six that unlock across chapters two to five. Each one should read as visibly made from its ingredients.

Customers

Twelve heads, paired arbitrarily with twenty-four names. A different stem — the camera is front-on and the framing is a bust, not a three-quarter object.

Machines and room

Six machines for the bin grid, plus the counter and the two backgrounds. The backgrounds use a wide aspect rather than a square.

Negative prompt

For Stable Diffusion, Flux and anything else that takes one. Midjourney users: append the --no line instead.

Tool settings

Midjourney

Best match for this painted casual style out of the box. Style reference is the whole game.

--ar 1:1 --style raw --sref <anchor url> --sw 100 --s 180
Stable Diffusion / ComfyUI

More work, more control, and the only route that gets genuinely repeatable results — train a LoRA on your first six accepted assets and the rest become easy.

IP-Adapter @ 0.6 · fixed seed · 1024×1024 · CFG 6–7
Flux / Ideogram / Firefly

Use the stem as written. Weaker style-reference tooling, so expect more drift and more regeneration. Firefly's licensing terms are the friendliest of the three for commercial use.

What to expect, honestly

Consistency is the whole problem, not the prompts. Expect to generate five to ten candidates per asset and keep one. Forty-one assets is realistically a few hundred generations and one to two weeks of iteration, not an afternoon.
Characters will be the worst of it. Food is rigid and describable; faces are neither. If after a day the twelve heads still do not look like one artist drew them, the pragmatic answer is to simplify the character style deliberately — flatter, less rendered, fewer facial details — rather than keep fighting for painted portraits.
Check the licensing before you build on it. Two separate questions: what your generator's terms permit commercially, and what Apple's and Google's policies require you to disclose about AI-generated content. Both change; check them at the time, not from memory.
A generated set will still read as less coherent than one artist's hand. That is the trade you are making for not commissioning anyone, and it is a reasonable one — but go in expecting "good enough that nobody comments" rather than a match for the screenshots you showed me.
